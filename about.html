About Us

The people behind EDSIL

Decades of educational leadership across the world, now focused on helping education navigate the AI transformation.

"Educational success lies not only in finding the unique path that each of us can best take through life, but in acquiring the courage, resilience and skills needed if we are to travel down that path in a way that makes a sustainable contribution to human thriving while allowing us a sense of ongoing personal fulfilment."

John Puddefoot

Founding Director and CEO

A graduate of Oxford and Edinburgh, John taught at Eton College for 25 years, as a Mathematics Master, Head of Mathematics and Deputy Head (Curriculum).

He then became Warden of S. Thomas' College, Mount Lavinia, Sri Lanka; the Founding Head of the Aga Khan Academy, Hyderabad, India; and the Founding Head of United World College, Dilijan, in the Republic of Armenia.

In recent years he has been involved in educational consultancy and start-ups in China, first with Sir Paul Judge, Bright Scholar Education, and later with RCE Suzhou and Dragon Spruce Changsha. He was the Honorary Principal of Xuzhou and Yiwu International Academies and the Founding Principal of BeeMee Primary School in Changsha, Hunan province.

Married, with three daughters and four grandchildren, John sees education as a key contributor to human progress and the establishment of aspirations that are sustainable.

"There is no end of good and well-meaning advice available, but it seldom takes sufficiently seriously the particular abilities, needs and interests of an individual, and too often young people are persuaded to do things for reasons that confer benefit on others rather than suit their particular talents. We created EDSIL to try to define a new kind of education that blends the very best traditions and practices of East and West and serves to facilitate a dynamic educational process that equips young people with all the best knowledge and wisdom from the past while also enabling them to create a sustainable future."

Susan Jesudason

Director, Author and Consultant

A graduate of La Trobe University in Australia and Victoria University in New Zealand, Susan has teaching qualifications from New Zealand and the UK.

As a Head of Humanities she created and implemented an integrated MYP humanities programme, and as an IBDP Co-ordinator she devised and implemented a full DP programme in the UWC. Her subjects include Religious Studies A Level, and Philosophy and Theory of Knowledge in the IB Diploma.

As an experienced university admissions counsellor she has established university advice departments in several schools and helped hundreds of students to find optimal university courses.

Her books on the IB Diploma Theory of Knowledge for Cambridge University Press, Decoding Theory of Knowledge, and Theory of Knowledge have been very successful, the latter a new edition of the classic ToK CUP text by Richard van de Lagemaat for the new ToK course.

Married, with two sons, a daughter, four grandchildren, a lively border collie and a perfectly-behaved German shepherd, Susan sees education as a key contributor to human progress and the establishment of aspirations that are humane, open-minded and sustainable.

"I have been a passionate dog-owner and trainer all my life, and one of the things I have learned from that is that the principles involved in getting co-operation from dogs are very similar to those required to inspire co-operative learning in students. Finding what young adults naturally want to do and then emphasising and reinforcing it is a far better approach to teaching and learning than trying to force them through some method of carrot and stick. Teaching is largely a matter of admitting that you are as much a learner as your students and creating an atmosphere in the classroom—and outside it—where everyone learns and everyone teaches."
